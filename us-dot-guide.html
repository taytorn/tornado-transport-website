01 / Know the difference

Safety registration and authority.

A USDOT Number is the identifier FMCSA uses to collect and monitor a company’s safety information from audits, compliance reviews, crash investigations and inspections. Registration requirements depend on what the business does, its vehicles and where it operates.

USDOT Number

Identifies the registered entity for safety oversight. Having a number alone does not establish that every requirement for operating has been met.

Operating authority

Determines the type of authorized interstate for-hire operation and cargo. It is often associated with an MC, FF or MX docket number. Some operations need authority in addition to a USDOT Number; others are exempt from authority requirements.

Sources: FMCSA USDOT requirements · Operating authority overview

02 / Check your operation

Who needs a USDOT Number?

In general, federal USDOT registration applies to commercial motor vehicles used on a highway to transport property or passengers in interstate commerce when at least one of these criteria is met:

  • Weight
    10,001 pounds or more.

    The gross vehicle weight rating, gross combination weight rating, gross vehicle weight or gross combination weight reaches this threshold, whichever is greater. Do not check the truck’s empty weight alone.

  • Passengers
    More than 8 for compensation, or more than 15 without compensation.

    The vehicle is designed or used to transport that number of people, including the driver. Compensation may be direct or indirect.

  • Hazmat
    Hazardous materials in a quantity requiring placarding.

    This interstate criterion can apply regardless of vehicle weight. Separately, intrastate carriers transporting types and quantities of hazardous materials that require a federal safety permit must also obtain a USDOT Number.

Interstate does not only mean crossing a state line yourself.A trip within one state can still be part of interstate commerce when the transportation originates or terminates outside the state. Trips between two places in a state through another state also count.

Operating only within one state?

Some states require USDOT Numbers for intrastate commercial operations. Check with the responsible state agency, and consider the hazardous-material safety-permit requirement above. Personal, farm or government use should not be treated as a blanket exemption without checking the rules for that specific operation.

Sources: USDOT requirements & interstate scope · FMCSA vehicle and passenger applicability · FMCSA USDOT FAQ

03 / Prepare, then register

A practical registration checklist.

FMCSA’s current registration resources identify Motus as the USDOT Registration System. Start from the official registration homepage and follow the latest guidance for a new or existing business, rather than older URS or Portal walkthroughs.

Have your business details ready.

  • Legal business name and any trade name
  • Business address and contact details
  • Business and operation type
  • Cargo or passenger-service details
  • Vehicle and driver information
  • Tax and identity information requested by FMCSA
  • Hazardous-material details, if applicable
  • Existing USDOT or authority records, if any
  1. 01

    Determine your registration needs.

    Review USDOT requirements, interstate operating authority, any hazardous-material safety permit and applicable state registrations. Define the operation accurately before selecting registrations.

  2. 02

    Use FMCSA’s current starting point.

    Open FMCSA Registration and the Motus resources hub. Follow the current account, identity-verification and company-access instructions for your role. Do not create a duplicate business record just to avoid an access problem.

  3. 03

    Complete and review the requested information.

    Use the business’s correct legal identity and the registration workflow appropriate to its existing records. Check the operation, cargo and contact information carefully before submitting sensitive information on the official government system.

  4. 04

    Separate USDOT registration from authority fees.

    FMCSA does not charge for the USDOT Number itself. Its published fee for each permanent operating authority requested is $300, non-refundable. Confirm the current fee and the authority you actually need. Other registrations, insurance or a private filing service can have separate costs.

  5. 05

    Submit and keep the official records.

    Save the confirmation and follow FMCSA’s instructions for tracking the application and responding to requests. This guide does not promise a number or an approval within a set number of days.

  6. 06

    Confirm everything required before operating.

    Do not treat an assigned USDOT Number as active operating authority. Check your registration and authority status, required insurance or financial-responsibility filings, process-agent designation where applicable and state requirements.

  7. 07

    Mark the vehicle correctly.

    For covered interstate self-propelled CMVs, display the carrier’s legal name or single trade name and identification number preceded by “USDOT” on both sides. The lettering must contrast sharply with the background and be legible in daylight from 50 feet. Review the marking rule and any operation-specific requirements; a blanket two-inch federal minimum is not the standard stated here.

Sources: Current registration resources · USDOT fee FAQ · Authority & fees · CMV markings

04 / Keep the record current

Registration is the beginning.

Safety and recordkeeping responsibilities continue after registration. The requirements that apply to your business depend on its operations and any applicable exceptions.

Company updates

Keep business information current. FMCSA ordinarily requires an update every two years, even if the information has not changed. Check the current update instructions and the transition notice below.

New entrant oversight

Covered new interstate motor carriers enter an initial 18-month monitoring period. Prepare for a safety audit and maintain safe operations throughout the program.

Required records

Maintain applicable driver qualification, hours-of-service, vehicle inspection and maintenance records, accident records and required drug/alcohol program records.

Ongoing safety review

FMCSA monitors safety performance through roadside inspections and other data. Compliance reviews or interventions may occur when safety information indicates problems.

Current Motus transition notice — check for updates.As of the November 24, 2026 source review, FMCSA’s published notice temporarily suspends biennial-update enforcement and USDOT inactivation for that failure. Registrants whose update was due on or after June 1, 2026 receive additional time. This is not a waiver of other safety or operating requirements. Read the official notice and follow any newer guidance.

Sources: Registration updates · New entrant program & safety duties

05 / Common questions

Before you apply.

There is no processing-time promise in this guide. Registration review, identity verification, follow-up information and system conditions can affect timing. An assigned identifier also does not mean any required authority is active. Use current FMCSA resources for status and next-step guidance.

FMCSA does not charge for the USDOT Number itself. Its published fee is $300 for each permanent operating authority requested, and authority application fees are non-refundable. A third-party service charge is separate from a government registration fee. Confirm the current charges before submitting.

A USDOT Number identifies the entity for safety oversight. “MC Number” commonly refers to a motor-carrier operating authority docket number. Many interstate for-hire operations need authority, but exemptions exist, including some exempt-commodity operations. Private carriers transporting their own cargo generally do not need interstate for-hire authority. Review FMCSA’s authority overview and current Motus registration changes for the status and identifiers used in your record.

Possibly. Some states require USDOT registration for intrastate operations. Some hazardous-material operations also have a federal registration requirement. A route within one state may still be part of interstate commerce based on the shipment’s origin and destination. Start with FMCSA’s requirements and check with your responsible state agency.

Use FMCSA’s current instructions for representatives and supporting companies. The business must authorize access, and the applicable account role and identity checks must be completed. This guide does not prescribe a universal power-of-attorney document. Consult the Motus guides or ask FMCSA before acting for another entity.
